# Working at Heights Procedure

## When this procedure applies

This procedure applies to any task performed where a person could fall two metres or more, including work on roofs, scaffolds, elevated work platforms, ladders above the threshold height, and work near unprotected edges, penetrations, or fragile surfaces.

A Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) is mandatory before any work at heights begins, because work at heights is classified as high risk construction work.

## Control hierarchy

Controls must be selected in this order: eliminate the need to work at height, use passive fall prevention such as guardrails and edge protection, use work positioning systems, and only then rely on fall arrest systems such as harnesses and lanyards. Administrative controls and PPE are the last line of defence, never the first.

## Harness and lanyard inspection

A full body harness must be visually inspected by the user before each use. Check webbing for cuts, fraying, chemical damage and UV degradation, check stitching for broken or pulled threads, and check buckles and D-rings for cracks, distortion and corrosion.

In addition to pre-use checks, harnesses and lanyards must receive a detailed inspection by a competent person at least every six months, and the inspection must be recorded in the equipment register with the inspector's name and date. Any harness that has arrested a fall must be immediately withdrawn from service and destroyed.

## Anchor points

Anchor points for fall arrest must be rated to at least 15 kN for a single person and must be installed or verified by a competent person. Anchors must be inspected before each use and load tested according to the manufacturer's schedule.

## Rescue plan

A rescue plan is required whenever fall arrest equipment is used. Suspension trauma can become life threatening within minutes, so the plan must not rely solely on emergency services response. The rescue plan must be documented in the SWMS and rehearsed with the crew before work begins.
