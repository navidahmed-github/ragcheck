# Incident Reporting and Investigation Procedure

## What must be reported

All incidents must be reported, including injuries, near misses, plant damage, environmental releases, and dangerous occurrences such as structural collapse or contact with energised services. Near miss reporting is treated with the same weight as injury reporting, because near misses are the cheapest learning the site will ever get.

## Reporting timeframes

Any injury or dangerous occurrence must be reported verbally to the site supervisor immediately, and an incident report must be entered into the incident management system within 24 hours of the event. Notifiable incidents, including serious injuries and dangerous incidents defined by the regulator, must be reported to the regulator immediately by the site manager, and the scene must be preserved until the regulator releases it.

## Investigation

Incidents are investigated in proportion to their actual and potential severity. High potential incidents receive a formal investigation led by a trained investigator using causal analysis, with findings reviewed at the monthly safety committee.

Investigations focus on absent or failed controls and on the conditions that made the error likely, not on blaming the individual. A report that names a person as the root cause is returned for rework.

## Corrective actions

Every investigation must produce corrective actions with a named owner and a due date, tracked to closure in the incident management system. Corrective actions that change a procedure or control must be communicated at the next toolbox talk, and their effectiveness must be verified ninety days after closure.

## Injury classification

Injuries are classified as first aid injury (FAI), medical treatment injury (MTI), restricted work injury (RWI), or lost time injury (LTI). Classification is made by the site manager with advice from the treating medical provider, and must not be influenced by reporting statistics targets.
