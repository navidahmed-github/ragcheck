# Scaffold Erection and Inspection Standard

## Scaffold classification

Scaffolds are classified by duty rating: light duty (225 kg per platform per bay), medium duty (450 kg), and heavy duty (675 kg). The required duty rating must be determined from the trades and materials expected on the platform and recorded on the scaffold plan before erection begins.

Any scaffold from which a person or object could fall more than four metres must be erected, altered and dismantled only by workers holding the appropriate scaffolding high risk work licence.

## Handover certificate

A scaffold must not be used until the scaffolding contractor has issued a handover certificate confirming the scaffold is complete and compliant with the design. The certificate must state the duty rating, the date of handover, and any limitations on use. Incomplete scaffolds must display "scaffold incomplete - do not use" signage at every access point.

## Inspection frequency

Scaffolds must be inspected by a competent person before first use, at intervals not exceeding 30 days while in service, after any alteration or repair, and after any event that could affect stability, including severe weather, impact by plant, or nearby excavation. Inspection outcomes are recorded on the scafftag at the access point and in the site scaffold register.

## Alterations

Only licensed scaffolders may alter a scaffold, including removing ties, braces, or planks. Trades must never remove scaffold components to gain access for their work. Any unauthorised alteration voids the handover certificate, and the scaffold must be re-inspected and re-certified before use resumes.

## Mobile scaffolds

Mobile scaffold castors must be locked whenever the scaffold is stationary. The platform height must not exceed three times the minimum base dimension unless outriggers are fitted, and mobile scaffolds must never be moved while a person or materials remain on the platform.
